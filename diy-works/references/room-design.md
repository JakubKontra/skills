# Fit out a furnished interior

Use the sections for the affected rooms. Treat each household preference, room assignment, material, product, and device as a project choice. Carry confirmed choices through later revisions. A request for an airy bedroom, for example, can be tested through actual floor space, sightlines, and usable access to storage.

## Read reference images and product data

- For each reference image, identify what the user points to: a floating desktop and chair, a bank of cabinets above a bed, a lit open shelf, or the finish on the fronts. If two images describe different parts of the room, map each one to its own assembly. Fit the idea to the measured room and retained openings. A reference photo supplies proportions and design intent, not verified millimetres.
- For a named product, match manufacturer, exact model or SKU, size, handedness, and country-specific variant where applicable. Prefer a supplied technical sheet or the manufacturer's drawing and native CAD/3D asset. Preserve its source, units, and reference orientation. Align a native asset with its 2D outline and inspect a characteristic asymmetric feature such as a drain position or curved rim. Confirm that both the plan symbol and the model show the selected product.
- Distinguish purchase dimensions from installation dimensions. For example, a mattress size is different from the external bed-frame footprint. A refrigerator's overall depth may include handles, and the body dimensions alone do not determine how far the doors must open to remove its drawers. If a component is provisional, identify the missing dimension or exact order code.

## Kitchens and adjoining service rooms

Start with the wall lengths between finished faces, openings, sill and ceiling heights, utilities, and the available circulation space. Use these to lay out the tall units, wet area, preparation surface, cooking area, dining zone, and adjacent storage.

- Draw actual modules with side panels, fillers, appliance housings, worktops, and door or drawer movement. Check the sequence from fridge to landing surface, washing, preparation and cooking, and the route from dishwasher to crockery storage. Quote usable continuous worktop length when comparing “more surface”.
- For a large side-by-side fridge, include the manufacturer's ventilation space, hinges, handles and required opening angles. Test access to drawers and the adjacent wall, tall unit and chairs. Design ceiling infill and side panels around the service requirements of the selected unit.
- When comparing a relocated oven and microwave tower, show appliance heights, an adjacent landing area, and what storage is gained or lost. In a blind corner, draw a reachable cabinet opening and the actual travel of a carousel or pull-out if proposed. Credit only accessible storage. Check the device against the neighbouring appliance and drawer fronts.
- On an island with an induction hob, place the hood or other extraction system explicitly. Coordinate its route and height with the selected appliance, ceiling, pendant and sightlines. Draw the back of the island as well as the working face, including any intended storage, seating overhang, and accessible service panels.
- At a coffee station, check usable counter length, headroom to refill the machine, sockets, and the underside of uppers. Align cabinet fronts, end panels, shelves, reveals and task lighting with the tall run. Identify under-cabinet or worktop lighting separately from decorative accents. Position wall accessories, including a knife rail if requested, against the actual reach and household brief.
- If a window is reduced to gain a cabinet bay, locate the retained jamb explicitly and calculate the usable bay after reveals, fillers, and end panels. Keep any separately removed window closed in later variants. For a kitchen-access pantry, check the door, shelf returns and turning space alongside the adjacent utility and WC. Check usable shelf depth and access to service equipment after changing partitions.

## Bathrooms and asymmetric fixtures

Identify the wet area, entry side, plumbing face and installation envelope. A walk-in shower may include one fixed screen, a level-entry floor, drain, wall-mounted mixer and shower fittings. Show which edge remains open, the clear entry width, and how the adjacent bath, basin or door can be used. If both showers share a feature, check each instance and its host wall independently.

For a recessed shelf, identify the host wall and establish available build-up. Where the exterior wall is to remain intact, model a separate room-side service lining, cut the recess only in that lining, and draw a section showing the backing that remains. Move the glass profile, fittings and floor or drain references to the new finished face as appropriate. Recalculate the clear shower width after lining and finishes. Use the selected system details for installation dimensions.

For an asymmetric bath such as the RAVAK Asymmetric, match the selected product code and compare handed variants from an explicitly named view. Show the actual outline, widest point, waste location, tap side and access panel. When it trades places with the shower, test both installed footprints and their access before updating the paired files.

## Bedrooms, wardrobes and study areas

- Base bed placement on the outer frame plus access for making the bed. If the brief specifies a 180 × 200 cm mattress or a named bed such as the LAURA oak bed, verify the corresponding frame variant and outer dimensions. Confirm the window being retained, clearance for opening it, the door approach, and access to each wardrobe or drawer. Choose age and future use from the brief. A room planned for a teenager and used initially by a young child may need separate present-use and future configurations.
- For modular wardrobes such as PAX, choose exact available frame widths, depths and heights, fronts, and interior fittings from the relevant catalogue. Plan the rail and shelf heights, drawers, side fillers, assembly and raising clearance, anchoring space, and door travel. Check how many complete modules fit. Document any custom bridging cabinetry over a bed as a separate assembly.
- Fit a dressing or study station to the space after any change to a window. Draw the desktop, chair in use, leg space, drawer clearance, mirror sightline, task lighting and accessible sockets. Above the bed, compare the projection and underside height of cupboards and an open niche with the headboard and sitting position. Show access to fasteners and lighting drivers.
- For an L-shaped desk, lay out the primary working arm and the return around the chair, bed and wardrobe. Establish drawer banks, knee bay, accessible inside corner and tabletop support before sizing the uppers. Where requested, coordinate closed cupboards, open niches, warm shelf lights, slatted panels, a pinboard and cable routing in an elevation. For fabrication, take the top profile and its inside radius from the approved geometry.

## Living and dining

Plot the actual dining table and all requested chairs, including the occupied and pulled-out positions. Keep sightlines between kitchen, dining and seating where the brief calls for them. Test the route to a terrace, window or hall with furniture in use.

Give a wood-burning insert its own footprint and operating area. Check the proposed flue route and openings against the selected installation documentation before treating an enclosure as buildable. For a wine fridge, preserve its ventilation and door travel. For a record player, show a stable surface, clearance for its lid, access to records, and power and signal routing. Place artwork and lighting against real walls and viewing positions. Assess a seasonal tree, play area, or other temporary use as a separate arrangement with an explicit position relative to the requested window and table.
